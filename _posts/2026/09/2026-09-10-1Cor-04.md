Paul always remind us what our Lord Jesus Christ taught, that we are servants of Christ and stewards of God’s mysteries, here he meant the gospel of Christ that’s meant to be shared with everyone including gentiles and not just a special faction. It is not cultish as if only some elites can attain this mystery, it is about the revelation of God gradually given to those who believe in Him and then being shared by Christians to unbelievers that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit to a virgin, Mary, without human’s intimate interaction, who then sacrificed on the cross for the sins of mankind, resurrected on the third day and is now seated at the right hand of God. 

Our Lord Jesus Christ appeared to Paul on the road to damascus which caused him to be blinded and then healed him thru Ananias. The point is that Paul experienced the encounter with Christ by revelation from God as he was a Pharisee and persecuted Christians because he truly believed that they were abominations to God and his zeal for God was not paired with this knowledge yet, hence the word mystery. A Christian’s testimony is about glorify God and how He has helped us, it’s never about how we could do something by ourselves, that’s a danger to self-righteousness and puffed up. As if a challenge to God, “Look here, God, You did nothing, I was the one that choose to do all that and now You owe me”. 

A seasoned pastor should learn to discern from the heart and if someone is in danger of being self-centered, one should gently speak of it and if they listen, then the person is won over for Christ, but if not, let it be and surrender them to God. This is what it meant by Paul’s mentioning of the boasting as if they did not receive. The testimony is just an example, but the principle applies to all appropriate situations, such as the faction issues in Corinth was due to striving against each other as if they were the one that did something to earn something and then used that against other faction so that they can feel superior than other but in fact it was a gift from God. Nobody earned the gift of Salvation, it was freely given by God. The promise of God was that it came out of the root of the Davidic bloodline, Joseph & Mary were both from that bloodline, which was given to Abraham. That was to emphasize the faithfulness of God even when there were weaknesses in the bloodline. The point is human is weak, God is God, human should always remain humble, not in a way that is self-demeaning but to see the reality that there is a God and we are the creation. 

Paul knew their pride was in the way and so he used a form of sarcasm in the hope that it will clash them into an awakening but it was brilliant because they strive as if to prove themselves, we all know that a lot of really rich men do not walk around and prove to everyone that they are rich. So in the same manner, the church in Corinth already think that they are kings and already reigning with Christ here on earth, but Paul knew that they are yet to reach that spiritual maturity, it was to highlight the already and not yet perspective. This method sometimes work because of the clashing of both drastic contrast and somehow will open the eyes of readers, hence the brilliancy of Paul’s warning here. 

Paul continued to prepare the hearts of the church in Corinth that he’s sending Timothy to them as someone to lead them and to remind them of the ways of Paul in Christ Jesus. The emphasis is this, Paul teach that in everywhere in every church, meaning that he did not participate in factions or sectarianism. Paul was the chosen one that walked in both worlds, the Old Testament and crossing over to New Testament and so he was able to lead both peoples to God in this mystery of the gospel of Christ. For he knew how they think and had lived both of the cultures and was transformed by Christ to not conformed to both worlds’ wisdom but be led by the Spirit of God. 

In his admonition, he was to visit them not to hear their talk but to see their effectiveness as Christian in Corinth. It’s about the walk and not the talk. That’s the meaning of power mentioned by Paul. Were they really transformed? The kingdom of God depends not on talk but on power. We as Christian must walk the talk else we become hypocrites, and the level of spiritual maturity will leak and show in a congregation, but this may not be a bad thing, for when one sees or acknowledge that one has yet to reach that maturity, then one can take the next step to reach that maturity. The saddest part is like the church of Corinth in the past where they thought that they have become mature but in fact they still need to drink milk… 

May our Lord Jesus Christ help us all to be humble and truly follow His steps as a Christian and strengthen us then help us to walk the talk.
