In this chapter, Festus was trying to please the Jews as well to send Paul to Jerusalem for a trial but Paul was able to appeal that he be trialed before the emperor. This was his mission to proclaim the gospel of Christ to the gentile’s emperor and the prominent men that will attend the trial. It’s became a huge evangelical meeting among the powerful political community. 

Those who opposed God tried to bring Paul down but God turned it all around and it became an opportunity for Paul to spread the gospel even more. This time to the king of gentiles, King Agrippa and his queen, Bernice. All those who were present should have heard what was going on in the whole commotion and the king knew that he must deal with this cautiously as this is not a matter in crime or against the law of Roman empire but of matter in theology disagreement in between the Jews & Paul. 

The context here though is because the Jews were afraid and was envious of our Lord Jesus Christ’s proclaiming that He is the Son of God and thru Him people receive salvation, reconciling with God, our Father in heaven. After crucifying Jesus Christ, they could not accept that Jesus Christ resurrected from death and when Paul kept teaching about our Lord Jesus Christ in the synagogues, many Jewish believed in our Lord Jesus Christ too. So they wanted to shut Paul up and destroy his ministry. 

It does not mean that theology matter is secondary to the law here because God’s commandment always supersede all laws but if one truly understand the principle in God’s commandment, one will find that it’s complimentary with justice and mercy that which even many of the law of a country can be aligned which is to protect the weak. However as usual, men will twist the meaning and use it to oppress or control others. So to be clear, it’s not an excuse nor a valid reason to cause any riot without any reason, here Paul was being trialed unjustly and he had to use this method. 

God’s providence was always involved in this plan but He did not cause it, He knew what’s in men’s hearts and He did not allow nor agree but He turned the situation into something better. Salvation to the gentiles.
