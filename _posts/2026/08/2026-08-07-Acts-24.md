At the time when Paul stood before Tertullus, supporting the high priest, Ananias and some elders, who accused him before Felix, the whole position of Paul reversed. Back then he stood also with the Jews accusing other Christians, treated them harshly and hated them. Now what he once loved became unlovable, what he once hate became what he loved. Someone who’s been converted drastically will mostly experience this in their lifetime. It is common as their mind and perspective being renew by the Holy Spirit and the truth that sanctified their thoughts and hearts. Finally, they could see clearly and able to discern what pleased God and what does not. It is why they became someone that could no longer be friends with the world they knew but to be separated out for the work of God and in the intention of leading maybe some of their family and former friends to the truth, that is the salvation thru our Lord Jesus Christ. 

This is what Paul was trying to do all the while without using violence but kept on pushing the truth with his eloquence and knowledge in the Jewish and Greek culture. Nothing can stop him from proclaiming the truth even when he was misunderstood, rejected, hated, accused wrongly, slander and called crazy. That doesn’t mean that he did not have any emotions though, just because that our Lord Jesus Christ or someone else had it worse doesn’t mean that his experience were invalid or small. There’s no comparison for someone in a hurting position, doing that by comparing their pain is like comparing apple and orange… it only adds on the pain to the person who are hurting. 

Healing from pain needs acknowledgement and validation, often times people searching for a permission to be heard and then to begin the process of healing. Families and friends, often times who they respect will let them down and block their healing because they wanted the more convenient way for them. Healing is messy and unpacking the can of worms means that they might have to take some responsibility on causing it, people don’t like it when they have to pay for the consequences or rather they feel helpless and want to escape from the responsibility. So it increase the trauma and pain for the person instead of getting healed. 

Paul, though, had something that we can learn from. He took responsibility in a way that he was humbled by Christ in the experience of the road to Damascus. He knew that it’s his consequences to take on but he also knew that some of the Jewish community truly believed that they were doing it the wrong way for God’s sake…some not so much. He too knew that himself was not someone good but tried his best to serve God in ways that he could, to undo his wrongs and to save as much soul as he can to please God. He repented from his old ways of persecuting Christians to spreading the Gospel of Christ, something that please God. This is repentance, not only to stop doing what was evil, but begin to do what’s righteous. This is the way of life giving to the community as follower of Christ. 

When the opportunity comes where Felix heard his teaching or discussion about justice, self-control and the coming judgment, he was afraid. However Felix wanted a bribe from Paul, remember Paul had social support from the church and how he was a tentmaker to support those who are weaker? Felix must have known that and wanted some of the free money. Felix was filled with greed as the same as many officials in the government. This speaks of how corrupted the Roman government was and it happens around the world no matter what country it is. So, as a speaker of God, a prophet, a pastor, a teacher of the word, what did Paul do? What did he say to a greedy and powerful government official? How does that change our perspective and our speech when facing someone similar? How much courage does one need? 

May God help us to stand firm and speak the truth no matter how hard the circumstances is and to give us courage to speak His words boldly in peace and in love.
