Paul continued to express his desire, that is the Jewish community can be saved, he testified that they have a zeal for God but it wasn’t by faith, they wanted to fulfill the law so that they can attain salvation, which only puffs them up when they were able to follow it. This does not mean that law is not good, for example, law of gravity exist on earth, so when water is poured, it flows down, it doesn’t flows up unless when there’s other force being introduce into the surround such as wind or force of air. This is just one of the example to bring out the fact that whatever we do, there are consequences. There’s no argument or debate in it but what Paul was trying to address in context of book of Romans is about the gift of salvation thru the grace of God by believing our Lord Jesus Christ in faith. Hence everyone who believes in their heart, and confess with their mouth will be saved! 

The gift of salvation is for all, Jews and Greeks / Gentiles, in other words, everyone on earth. Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved. The Jewish community has experienced God supernaturally from the time of Abraham to Moses, Elijah, Jonah until Jesus Christ. Somehow that creates the sense of entitlement in them and they fall into deception that they earned that because they were of the same bloodline or religion or connection in the sense of networking. It’s true though that the Israelites were the chosen one for God promised Abraham and no matter what happens, we can see throughout history how disobedient and wicked some could be, yet God still fulfilled His promise, this just show that we can trust in God’s promises. However this takes times, trust is earned and we all know once it’s broken, it often becomes nearly impossible to restore. We all learned the hard way so that we do not repeat the same mistake again. That’s not a bad thing, else all will always cross the line and commit even worse crime. So God chose not to break His promises and what does that tells us? He wants this to work and He is willing to bear all the bad in order to save mankind. So He sent Jesus Christ or in other words, Jesus Christ came down to earth willingly from Heaven to die for our sins so that we can be reconciled to God when we believed in Him! 

From the records of history, we all know that God is also just as He is merciful, the Jewish community had experienced so much injustice, hardship and suffering just because of who they are. Shouldn’t we then acknowledge that they too were not spared from the consequences of breaking the commandments of God? When they were cruel to others, the consequences comes back. Surely we all experience that in life, what goes around, comes around. No one is spared. If we can acknowledge that, shouldn’t we also acknowledge that gentiles were also cruel and unjust? Is there any race at all on earth that is as perfect as Jesus Christ, the Son of God? Sins were not offset just because the consequences happened, someone has to atone for it, Jesus Christ accomplished that. This was what the Jewish community struggles with during the time of Paul maybe even now. Even so, God is still continuing to work in His ways to provide the gift of salvation to them and we are also responsible as follower of Christ to join in prayers or ways that we are able for the Jewish community to be saved as well as the gentiles. May God open their eyes and their hearts that they may see and accept Jesus Christ as their Lord and Saviour.
